# Complete article authoring workflow

## Brief

Translate a topic into a small editorial contract before researching:

- audience: who should understand or act after reading;
- outcome: explain, compare, persuade, announce, recruit, or teach;
- scope: what the article covers and deliberately excludes;
- article type and tone;
- approximate length and delivery deadline;
- research depth and image policy.

Do not ask for fields the user has already implied. For example, “给产业读者解释一篇机器人论文” implies an accessible technical-paper article with standard research and a restrained research-oriented visual theme.

## Outline before prose

Each section must answer one reader question. A useful default sequence is:

1. Open with the concrete change, conflict, or reader problem.
2. State the central conclusion in plain language.
3. Explain background only to the depth needed.
4. Present the mechanism, evidence, comparison, or steps.
5. Separate verified results from interpretation and limitations.
6. Close with implications or a specific next action.

Avoid universal filler headings such as “前言” and “总结” when a more informative heading is possible. Avoid repeating the title in the first paragraph.

## Drafting rules for mobile reading

- Prefer 2–5 sentences per paragraph and one central idea per paragraph.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs; remove stacked adjectives and empty “重磅/颠覆/遥遥领先” language unless it is a clearly attributed quote.
- Introduce an acronym once before using it alone.
- Put the meaning of a metric next to the metric; do not dump numbers without a comparison baseline.
- Use lists when items are genuinely parallel, not to fragment every sentence.
- Use quotations only when the exact wording matters and the source is available.
- End sections with a transition that earns the next section.

## Rewrite modes

- `professional`: concise, evidence-led, neutral confidence.
- `academic`: explicit definitions, methods, limitations, and citations.
- `accessible`: plain-language analogy followed by the precise term.
- `sharp`: thesis-led analysis; distinguish argument from fact.
- `narrative`: scene, person, or chronological change, without fictionalizing.

## Revision passes

Run separate passes instead of attempting everything at once:

1. factual pass: claims, dates, names, metrics, quotations, citations;
2. structural pass: headline promise, section order, missing explanation;
3. language pass: redundancy, ambiguity, paragraph length, transitions;
4. visual pass: every image has an editorial job and correct placement;
5. publication pass: title, summary, metadata, alt text, credits, HTML QA.
