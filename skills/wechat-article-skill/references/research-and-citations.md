# Research and citation policy

Research is required when an article depends on current facts, outside claims, direct quotations, product specifications, published results, laws, schedules, prices, or named people and organizations. It is optional for a purely supplied personal essay unless the user requests fact checking.

## Research depth

- `none`: use only user-supplied material; state the boundary.
- `light`: verify the core entity and 2–4 pivotal claims.
- `standard`: verify every material factual section with primary or authoritative sources.
- `deep`: compare primary evidence, limitations, conflicting interpretations, and reputable secondary context.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. original paper, official project page, repository, dataset, regulation, filing, press release, product documentation, or direct transcript;
2. recognized public institution, standards body, university, or authoritative dataset;
3. reputable reporting or expert analysis that links to evidence;
4. aggregators and social posts only as leads, not sole proof of important claims.

Search snippets are not evidence. Open the source page. Record title, publisher, URL, publication date when available, access date, source type, and the claim it supports.

## Claim ledger

For every claim that could be challenged, store:

- a stable claim ID;
- the exact paraphrased claim;
- `verified`, `inference`, or `unverified`;
- one or more declared source URLs;
- notes about scope, denominator, benchmark conditions, or disagreement.

“Verified” means the opened source directly supports the wording. If the conclusion is derived from multiple sources, mark it as an inference and explain the reasoning. Remove or visibly qualify unverified material before publication.

## Writing citations

- Link a claim near the sentence it supports, or use numbered references when inline links would disrupt reading.
- A final `参考资料` section may list source title, publisher, date, and link.
- Never fabricate a quotation. Keep direct quotations short and respect source limits; prefer paraphrase.
- Do not cite an article for a claim it merely repeats if the primary source is available.
- Use explicit dates for time-sensitive claims so the article does not imply permanent truth.

## Conflict handling

When sources disagree, do not silently choose the most convenient number. State the difference, explain likely reasons such as methodology or timing, and avoid false precision. A confident visual presentation must not hide uncertain evidence.
